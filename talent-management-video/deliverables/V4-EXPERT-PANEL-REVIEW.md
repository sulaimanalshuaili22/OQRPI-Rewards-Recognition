# OQ RPI Talent Management film (v4): Expert Panel Assessment

**Reviewed:** `OQRPI-Talent-Management-v4-1080p` · 4:39 · 1920×1080 · 30 fps
**Method:** a frame-by-frame review (one frame every 3 s plus full-resolution checks), the narration script (`src/film/script.json`), the cut timeline, the on-screen data source (`src/film/data.ts`) and the audio pipeline (`tools/voiceover.py`, `tools/score.py`).
**Limit:** the panel could not do a real-time listening pass. The audio findings come from the production pipeline and measured loudness, not from a listening session in a room.

---

## The verdict in one paragraph

v4 is an **accurate, disciplined and honest internal briefing film**. Its data integrity is better than most Fortune 500 HR films: it gives as-of dates, separates DELIVERED from PLAN, and says outright that "named is not ready". It is **not yet a conference film**. Under the ecosystem wrapper it is still a **list of ten programmes**. It is narrated by a **synthetic voice** over a **General-MIDI orchestral score**. Its emotional climax ("It is built by people") plays over **particle clouds and an untextured CG refinery**. Not one employee speaks, and no one is named. It also has visible defects that a conference judge would catch in the first minute. Overall: **5.0 / 10**. The content foundation is strong enough to reach 8.5+, but only with a structural rebuild, not another polish pass.

## 1. Strategic Impact Review: 5.5 / 10

**What works.** The proposition "Protecting critical capability · Building future leaders · Advancing Omani talent" is the right one: three outcomes a CEO already owns. The business-challenge questions at 0:33–0:38 ("Which roles are critical? Who is ready? Where must capability be built?") are the best strategic moment in the film. The data discipline (as-of dates, status chips, "named is not ready") is something boards trust.

**What fails.** Every figure in the film is an **input or an activity**:

- 680 rated, 309 critical roles mapped, 237 MASAR alumni, 1,369 recognitions, 9 secondments
- **Zero outcomes:** no internal fill rate, no critical-role vacancy days avoided, no retention of high-potentials, no cost of an unplanned vacancy, no safety link, no time-to-readiness, no Omanization-rate movement, no promotion-from-within ratio.

A CEO hears "we did a lot of HR". A board member hears "what did it change?" The film never answers.

**Why should each audience care? The film should say it in their words:**

| Audience | What they actually worry about | What the film should show |
|---|---|---|
| CEO | "If my Head of Operations resigns tomorrow, who runs the plant?" | Critical-role coverage, ready-now successors, and the cost of one unplanned vacancy |
| Board | Continuity risk, national mandate (Oman Vision 2040), governance | Risk heat-map, nationalization trajectory, audit-grade data |
| Business VP | "Will I get the people I need for the turnaround and the expansion?" | Time-to-fill critical roles, internal fill rate, readiness by asset |
| Future leaders | "Is there a real path for me?" | One real person's path, and promotions made from the pipeline |

**Recommendations**
1. Add **three outcome metrics** that the Talent Command Center can already compute or derive, for example **internal fill rate for critical roles**, **high-potential retention**, and **critical roles promoted from within in 2026**. If they are not available yet, say "baseline set, 2027 target X". A target is an outcome statement.
2. Translate one number into money or risk: "An unplanned vacancy in a critical operations role costs the business an estimated OMR X and Y weeks of exposure." One sentence like this changes how the whole room reads the film.
3. Tie the film explicitly to **OQ strategy and Oman Vision 2040** with one line and one visual. Today it is implied.

## 2. Storytelling Review: 4.5 / 10

**Diagnosis: A) a list of initiatives.**

The ecosystem framing (scene 03) is a real improvement, but scenes 04–12 still run as a conveyor belt. Every chapter has the same anatomy:

> chapter tag → abstract 3D metaphor → data card → one definitional sentence → next chapter

| Scene | Length | Function |
|---|---:|---|
| 01 Opening | 22.6 s | Mood |
| 02 Why | 29.2 s | **Challenge (strong)** |
| 03 Ecosystem | 21.7 s | Model |
| 04–11 Eight programmes | 114.6 s | **Definitions, about 14 s each, identical rhythm** |
| 12 Command Center | 28.6 s | Re-lists all seven programmes |
| 13 Connections | 26.3 s | Re-walks the six-stage cycle |
| 14 Future | 35.9 s | Close |

The ecosystem is explained **three times**: in scene 03, by the Command Center node list in scene 12, and by the six-stage cycle in scene 13. That is about 75 s, a quarter of the film, spent saying "it is connected".

**There is no protagonist, no conflict and no turning point.** The energy is flat from 1:10 to 3:40. The music builds, but the story does not.

### Rebuilt story structure (target 3:00 hero cut)

**Concept: "The Handover."** One real critical role, one real person, one real successor. Every programme appears as a **tool in that person's journey**, not as a chapter.

| Act | Time | Beat | Content |
|---|---|---|---|
| **I. The Stakes** | 0:00–0:35 | Cold open | 4:00 a.m., the control room, a radio crackle. A shift supervisor at the board. VO: *"There are 309 roles in this company that can never be empty. Not for a week. Not for a night."* |
| | | The risk | The plant pulls back to aerial and 309 points light up across the site. *"Two years ago, we could not have told you who was next for most of them."* |
| **II. The System** | 0:35–1:50 | KNOW | Performance and the Talent Review, shown as **a real calibration room** (leaders around a table), not a floating grid. The hero number: **43 ready today**. |
| | | GROW | Our protagonist enters MASAR, then ROBBAN (the real cohort photo), then a secondment. An IDP appears as a single line. |
| | | SECURE | The expat mentor and the Omani successor work side by side. **Nationalization is shown as a handover between two people, not a timeline.** 242 roles to 2030. |
| | | SUSTAIN | Recognition: a real Testahal or Above & Beyond moment (a handshake, a certificate, a colleague's reaction). |
| **III. The Proof** | 1:50–2:35 | One system | The Command Center is shown live **once**. A leader asks the TM Assistant a real question in Arabic and gets the answer. |
| | | Outcomes | Three outcome numbers, large: continuity, leaders and national talent, each with a target. |

## 3. Script Review

**Measured facts**
- 424 words. "**Talent**" appears **16 times** (one word in 26). "OQ RPI" appears 10 times, "leader(s)" 10, "future" 6, "capability" 6, "success" 6.
- About 120 wpm while speaking, with narration over 76% of the runtime. It is close to wall-to-wall, with almost no silence for images to land.
- Seven of the fourteen scenes open with "[Programme name] + verb + us to…". That is definitional, glossary-style writing.

**Generic corporate wording**, the lines that could belong to any company:

| Current | Problem |
|---|---|
| "Every successful organisation shares one common strength… its people." | The most common opening line in HR films worldwide. It costs the first 10 seconds. |
| "behind every operation, every innovation, and every achievement, lies talent." | A rhetorical triplet with no content. |
| "build sustainable workforce capability, strengthen our leadership pipeline, and secure the future of our business" | Three abstract nouns. Nobody remembers them. |
| "enables us to assess, measure, and recognise employee contribution while supporting strategic talent decisions" | A policy-document register. |
| "The OQ RPI Talent Command Center is the central platform that brings it all together: Performance Management, Succession Planning, Critical Roles, Leadership Development, Nationalization, Secondment, and Rewards and Recognition, giving every leader real-time visibility." | **The weakest line in the film.** It is 36 words and re-lists seven programme names the audience has just heard. |
| "transforming data into decisions, potential into capability, and employees into future leaders" | A chiasmus formula that sounds like a template. |

**What is good and should stay:** "Not every position carries the same level of risk." · "Named is not ready." · "The future is not built by systems. It is built by people." · "Building tomorrow's talent, together."

### Recommended executive-level rewrite (hero cut, about 280 words)

> **[Cold open, control room, night]**
> There are three hundred and nine roles in this company that can never be empty.
> Not for a week. Not for a night.
>
> **[Aerial, points light up]**
> Talent Management at OQ RPI exists to answer three questions the business cannot afford to get wrong.
> Which roles matter most? Who is ready to take them? And what must we build before we need it?
>
> **[Calibration room]**
> It starts with an honest conversation: a performance review for every professional, and once a year, our leaders in one room, deciding who will lead us next.
> This year, forty-three are ready today.
>
> **[Protagonist, MASAR → ROBBAN → secondment]**
> Then we build them. Two hundred and thirty-seven leaders through MASAR since 2023. Twenty-four more in ROBBAN this October. Secondments across OQ and the nation that bring new capability home.
>
> **[Mentor and successor, side by side]**
> And we plan every handover. By 2030, two hundred and forty-two roles held today by international experts will be led by Omanis, each one trained by the expert they succeed.
>
> **[Recognition moment]**
> When people go above and beyond, we notice. One thousand three hundred and sixty-nine times this year.
>
> **[Command Center, Arabic query]**
> All of it lives in one place, live, for every leader. In English, or in Arabic.
>
> **[CEO / VP on camera, 1 line, their own words]**
>
> **[Outcomes]**
> Today, a hundred and thirty-two of our critical roles have a named successor. Our target is every one.
>
> **[Hand-over shot]**
> The future is not built by systems. It is built by people.
> OQ RPI Talent Management. Building tomorrow's talent, together.

Everything above reuses figures already in the film, except the coverage target, which needs owner sign-off.

## 5. Emotional Impact Review: 3.5 / 10

| Emotion | Present? | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Pride | Partial | The ROBBAN cohort photo, the Omani engineers, the nationalization roadmap |
| Trust | **Yes** | Status chips, as-of dates, "named is not ready" |
| Confidence | Partial | Numbers, but no outcomes |
| Inspiration | **No** | No story and no person |
| Human connection | **No** | Nobody speaks. Nobody is named. The only "journey" is labelled *"Illustrative example, not a specific employee."* |
| Future optimism | Weak | The future is shown as particle clouds and an orange sky |

**The core contradiction:** the film's thesis is *"It is built by people"*, yet about **70% of the screen time is abstract CG** (grids, particles, helices, rings, primitive refinery) and **0% is people speaking**. The audience is told to feel something about people while being shown geometry.

**Recommendations**
1. **One real protagonist** (with consent): an Omani engineer who came through MASAR and is now a named successor. Two lines of their own voice, in Arabic with English subtitles, is worth more than the whole Performance chapter.
2. **The mentor–successor pair**, filmed together. The expat expert talking about the person replacing them is the most moving and most Omani story Talent Management can tell.
3. **A recognition moment with a real reaction**: a colleague being surprised by an Above & Beyond award, filmed vérité.
4. **Remove the word "Illustrative"** by using a real, consenting journey.
5. **The CEO's voice.** One sentence, unscripted if possible.

## 9. Motion Graphics Review: 5.5 / 10

**Benchmark position**

| Reference | Their motion language | v4 gap |
|---|---|---|
| Apple | Extreme restraint, one idea per frame, huge type, perfect easing | v4 crowds frames with small UI and has several clipping errors |
| Microsoft (Build/Ignite) | Product UI as hero, real interface, clean depth | v4 recreates UI as stylised cards instead of showing the real product |
| Deloitte / McKinsey | Data as argument: one chart, one insight, an annotated "so what" | v4 shows data as decoration in cards |
| Adobe MAX | A bold kinetic-type system with a signature transition | v4 has no signature move |
| WEF | Real-world footage with restrained infographic overlays and global framing | v4 builds an abstract world instead of annotating the real one |

**Strengths:** a consistent palette (OQ orange plus teal); a clean card system; a good stage-tracker device on chapter tags; the nationalization roadmap and medal row are well built; the ecosystem "scatter → assemble" is a good idea.

**Weaknesses**
- **Cliché vocabulary:** DNA helix, Tron grid floor, particle "people clouds", glowing rings, light pillars, and arcs between particle clusters. Juries have seen each of these hundreds of times.
- **Type scale:** body text at about 9–14 px will not read on stage. Conference rule: nothing under **32 px at 1080p**, and hero numbers at 120 px or larger.
- **Primitive 3D:** untextured meshes without materials, AO or atmospherics.
- **Perspective data** (9-box at a steep angle) sacrifices readability for depth.
- **No signature transition or motif** that would make the film recognisable in a two-second clip.

**Recommendations**
1. **Adopt one motif: the OQ "slash" mark** (the orange, grey and teal parallel stripes on the opening and closing cards). Use it as the transition wipe, the progress line, the chapter divider and the data-highlight underline. That makes the motion language unmistakably OQ.
2. **Tracked AR overlays on real footage** in place of the CG world: critical-role pins on real units, successor names attached to real people (with consent).
3. **Data choreography:** one number at a time, a large count-up, then a short annotation line that says what it means.
4. **Real UI:** a screen-recorded Command Center with a cinematic camera (a slow dolly across a real monitor, then a screen-replace).

## 11. Brand Review: 6.0 / 10

## 12. International Conference Review: 3.5 / 10

**Would it stand out next to Microsoft, ADNOC, Aramco, Shell, BP, Siemens, Schneider, Deloitte or Accenture?** **No, not in its current form.**

**Why not**
1. **Synthetic narration.** The narrator is an open-source TTS model (`tools/voiceover.py`, Kokoro). On a conference PA, synthetic prosody is noticeable within one sentence, and it signals "low budget" before the content can earn respect. The engineering is impressive, but sampled GM strings and piano sound like a demo on a large system. Flagship films use a licensed library score at minimum, and often an original recorded score.
3. **CG fidelity.** The primitive 3D refinery is far below the photoreal CG or real drone cinematography that energy majors use.
4. **No human testimony.** Their talent films are almost always built on real employees speaking.

**What would get it there:** a human narrator (or real voices), a licensed or recorded score, real footage replacing the CG world, a protagonist story, a CEO voice, a bilingual version, a 3:00 cut, and zero defects. None of these needs a Hollywood budget; together they are a two-to-three-day shoot plus post.

**What would make it *stand out*:** the honesty. No energy major shows "named is not ready" or a 132-of-309 coverage gap with a plan to close it. **Candour is a differentiator at conferences full of self-congratulation.** Keep it.

## 13. Creative Director Review: unlimited budget

**The big idea: "THE HANDOVER"**
A film about the moment knowledge passes from one person to another, which is exactly what Talent Management exists to protect.

**Signature moments**

1. **"309 Lights."** A night drone over the real Sohar complex. One by one, **309 points of light** switch on across real units, one for each critical role. Then **132 turn from white to OQ orange** ("we know who's next") and the rest pulse softly ("our work for the next two years"). It is a data visualisation built onto the real asset, and it is the shot people will remember.

2. **"The Two Hands."** An extreme close-up on a control-room console. The weathered hand of a senior expatriate engineer rests on a control. A younger Omani hand moves in beside it. The older hand lifts away. It is held in silence, with no music for three seconds. This is the film's emotional peak and its nationalization story in one image.

3. **"The Room."** A real Talent Review calibration session, filmed vérité with consent and nothing confidential on screen. Leaders lean in, a pen taps the 9-box printout, someone says "she's ready." No film like this shows the decision being made, and that makes the 9-box human.

4. **"Ask it in Arabic."** A real VP at a real desk types *"كم عدد القادة الجاهزين اليوم؟"* ("How many leaders are ready today?"). The TM Assistant answers instantly, the screen pulls out into a real-time Command Center, and the camera flies through it. It is the AI moment that conference audiences photograph.

5. **"Mirror Cut."** The MASAR 2023 cohort photo dissolves into the same people standing in the same positions today, in their new roles and promotions labelled. Real, time-based proof of development.

6. **"The Unbroken Shot."** A 40-second Steadicam oner: it starts on a graduate's first day at the gate, moves through a training room, a control room, a ROBBAN session and a boardroom, and the person changes at each threshold (graduate → engineer → supervisor → leader). It shows the talent pipeline as one continuous movement through the real plant.

7. **The ending.** The camera rises from the two hands, up through the control-room ceiling, out into the dawn over the plant, with 309 lights fading into sunrise. *"The future is not built by systems. It is built by people."* Black. Logo. Silence.

**What the audience should say:** *"That's the first time I've seen succession planning make me feel something."*
